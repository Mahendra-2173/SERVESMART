## 3. Direct Statements from Cook Rameshwar Prasad

During unstructured kitchen interviews and daily debriefings, Ramesh Bhaiya articulated the exact challenges of his daily preparation decisions:

1. **On Peak Friday Rush & Student Frustration:**
   > *"Friday shaam ko ladke labs se aate hain toh unko garam samosa turant chahiye hota hai. Agar 6:30 baje khatam ho gaya toh jhagda karte hain aur bolte hain ki mess wale chor hain."*  
   > *(Translation: "On Friday evenings, students finish labs and rush directly here; they expect piping hot samosas immediately. If food runs out before 6:30 PM, they argue aggressively and accuse the mess staff of negligence.")*

2. **On Weather Sensitivity:**
   > *"Baarish hoti hai toh chai aur pakora ka demand achanak double ho jata hai, par agar tez toofan aa jaye toh hostel ke kamron se koi nikalta hi nahi."*  
   > *(Translation: "When it drizzles, the craving for tea and pakoras shoots up instantly; but if a sudden torrential thunderstorm strikes, nobody steps out of their rooms and prepared trays sit cold.")*

3. **On the Flaw of Rule-of-Thumb Guesswork:**
   > *"Main pehle andaze se banata tha ki chalo roz 90 plate bana lo. Par somwar ko 15 samosa bach ke fekna padta tha, aur shukrawar ko 80 par hi khatam ho jata tha. Koi hisab nahi tha."*  
   > *(Translation: "Earlier I just guessed 90 portions every day by routine. But on Mondays, 15 samosas were leftover and had to be dumped, whereas on Fridays we ran out at 80 portions. There was no real calculation behind it.")*

## 4. Surprising Fieldwork Observation: The "Rain Paradox"

A widely held assumption prior to fieldwork was: *"Rain always increases snack demand."*

Fieldwork data revealed a fascinating non-linear bifurcation:

- **Light Drizzle / Overcast Weather:**  
  When light drizzle occurred around 4:30–5:30 PM (e.g., Oct 8 and Oct 13), students readily walked to the ground floor mess for hot pakoras and tea. Turnout surged to 92–96 students, and snack portions **sold out completely (100% stockout rate)**.
- **Torrential Downpours / Storms:**  
  When heavy, relentless downpours occurred around 5:00 PM (e.g., Oct 7), students remained stuck in their department laboratories 1.2 kilometers away. The 5:00–6:00 PM entrance headcount dropped to 38 students. Because Ramesh Bhaiya had started frying 85 portions assuming "it's raining so people will eat", 24 portions remained completely unsold by 7:00 PM closure.

**Core Design Lesson:** Weather cannot be evaluated in isolation. Early turnout headcount (5:00 PM – 6:00 PM) serves as the indispensable physical ground truth that reveals whether students actually made it back to the hostel or remain trapped off-site.
